What Cable TV Actually Costs in Canada in 2026

Canadian cable providers charge some of the highest rates in the world. The CRTC (Canadian Radio-television and Telecommunications Commission) requires providers to offer skinny basic packages starting around $25/month — but anyone who has tried to watch NHL hockey on a basic package knows that you need to add multiple sports tiers before you can watch a single Maple Leafs game.

Cable Pricing Reality Check

Cable providers in Canada frequently advertise promotional pricing (e.g., "$89.99/month for 12 months") that increases dramatically after the promotional period ends. The real cost over a 2-year contract can be 40–60% higher than the advertised price.

The Hidden Fees That Make Cable Even More Expensive

The monthly package price is just the beginning. Canadian cable subscribers routinely pay additional fees that aren't prominently advertised:

  • Equipment rental: Set-top box rental adds CA$10–20/month per box — so a family with 3 TVs pays CA$30–60/month just for boxes
  • Installation fees: New installations often cost CA$99–150 upfront
  • 4K/HD upgrade fees: Many providers charge extra for their 4K tier
  • Early termination fees: Breaking a 2-year cable contract can cost CA$200–400
  • Annual price increases: Cable providers routinely increase prices 5–10% per year, with minimal notice
  • Pay-per-view events: Major events like boxing PPV or UFC add CA$50–80 per event
