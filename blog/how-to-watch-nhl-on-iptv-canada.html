NHL Channels in Canada — Complete Breakdown

Unlike the United States where NHL rights are split between TNT and ESPN, Canada's NHL broadcasting landscape is dominated by two major groups — Bell Media's TSN and Rogers' Sportsnet. Understanding which channel carries which game is key to never missing your favourite team.

TSN (The Sports Network) — 5 Channels

TSN 1: Pacific coast games — Canucks, Flames away games, west-coast matchups.
TSN 2: Quebec-focused — Canadiens games in English, plus eastern conference matchups.
TSN 3: Manitoba/prairie-focused — Jets games, midwest matchups.
TSN 4: Ontario focused — Maple Leafs alternate broadcasts, east coast games.
TSN 5: Senators and overflow games — senators, late-night west coast games in the east.

Sportsnet — 5 Regional Channels

Sportsnet East: Atlantic Canada games, Ottawa Senators home games, east coast matchups.
Sportsnet Ontario: Toronto Maple Leafs — primary home for Leafs Nation across Ontario.
Sportsnet West: Calgary Flames, Edmonton Oilers, and prairie province coverage.
Sportsnet Pacific: Vancouver Canucks — the go-to channel for BC hockey fans.
Sportsnet 360: Overflow NHL games, international matchups, pre/post-game shows.

French-Language NHL Coverage

For Quebec viewers, the Montreal Canadiens are broadcast in French on RDS (Réseau des sports) for regular season games and TVA Sports for nationally broadcast games. French-language commentary makes games significantly more engaging for francophone fans — and IPTV Canada includes both channels.

CBC — Hockey Night in Canada

Saturday nights still belong to Canada with Hockey Night in Canada on CBC. These nationally broadcast Saturday games are free over-the-air in Canada, but IPTV makes it easy to watch on any device without antenna limitations.
